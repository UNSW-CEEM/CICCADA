The same counterfactual was applied to Volt-Watt across the residential-scale cohort (n=1,580). Of these, 573 sites (36.3%) were exposed to Volt-Watt at least once, defined as an average site voltage above 253 V, and 329 sites (20.8% of the cohort; 57.4% of those exposed) showed an effective loss of energy. The total estimated Volt-Watt curtailment was 5.548 MWh, equivalent to 2.076% of modelled PV generation over the exposed intervals, or 11.808% of the exposed timestamps. Of that total, 49.6% (2.752 MWh) was mandated by the standard's power ceiling and 50.4% (2.795 MWh) was reduction beyond that requirement. Only the second component represents inverter behaviour that the standard did not ask for. The loss is similarly concentrated: the top 1% account for 17%, the top 5% account for 43%, the top 10% account for 63%, and the top 20% account for 85% of the total. Unlike the Volt-VAr figures, this population is disjoint by construction: Volt-VAr is assessed over 240-253 V and Volt-Watt only above 253 V, so the two totals are additive rather than overlapping. This is also a lower bound -- intervals without a counterfactual contribute zero rather than an unknown, and the counterfactual covers 95.8% of exposed intervals.
