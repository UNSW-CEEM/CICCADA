CICCADA Plot Colours

The colours and conventions used in every CICCADA figure on inverter conformance with AS/NZS 4777.2:2020. A given colour means the same thing in every chart, paper and report, so a reader who learns it once can read any figure.

Principles

One colour, one meaningVoltage is always vermilion, active power always green, reactive power always blue. No colour is reused for anything else.
Rules are blackRequirements from the standard (curves, ceilings, permitted bands) are drawn in black with dashed lines, so a rule never looks like data.
One scale for conformanceReds mean too little response, blue means conformant, purple means too much. Grey means not judged.
Readable without colourQuantity colours come from the Okabe–Ito colour-blind-safe set. Line style, weight and hatching carry meaning as well as hue.

Measured quantities

Used whenever a line or point shows one physical quantity.

Phases

Three-phase sites follow the red / white / blue phase convention. White is drawn light grey so it shows on a white page. Each phase is a thin line; the thick line drawn over them is the quantity colour (average voltage, or site total power).

L1, red#C00000 thin
L2, white#B0B0B0 thin
L3, blue#1F3A93 thin
Average or totalquantity colour, thick

Data source

When a panel already fixes the quantity and several measurements of it are compared, colour and line style identify where the measurement came from.

Inverter or PV-circuit monitoring (reference)#0072B2 solid, filled markers
Household net meter#E69F00 dashed, hollow markers
Earlier method, for comparison#999999 dotted, square markers

Unordered groups (network, manufacturer, cohort) use Matplotlib's petroff10 colour sequence, assigned once so each group keeps its colour across figures. "Other" and "Unknown" are always #999999.

Site-day figures

One site, one day. Stacked panels share a 00:00–24:00 time axis in Australian Eastern Standard Time (fixed UTC+10, no daylight saving).

  • Panel order: voltage, active power, Volt-Watt non-conformance, reactive power, Volt-VAr non-conformance. Figures for one mode keep the panels they need, in the same order.
  • Units: P and Q as % of the inverter rating on the left axis, kW and kvar on the right axis (the same scale, relabelled).
  • Fixed ranges so days can be compared: V 215–265 V, P −5 to 115 %, Q −70 to +55 %, widened only when the data go outside them.
  • Sign convention: generator convention of AS/NZS 4777.2. Positive Q is supplying, negative Q is absorbing.
  • Not assessable: below 20 % of rated power the standard sets no quantified minimum reactive capability. Those intervals are hatched grey and left out of every total.
  • Three-phase sites: each phase as % of a third of the rating, so the thick average line equals the site total as % of rating. Conformance is judged on the thick line.
